---

## Overview

In the summer and autumn of **1356**, **Edward of Woodstock**, known as the **Black Prince**, led a large-scale mounted raid (*chevauchée*) from English-held Gascony into the heart of the French kingdom. The campaign aimed to plunder the countryside, undermine French royal authority, and draw the army of King **John II** into a pitched battle. It ended spectacularly on **19 September 1356** with the **Battle of Poitiers**, where the French king himself was captured.

## The Campaign

### Nature of a Chevauchée
A *chevauchée* was a mobile raid focused on:
- **Devastation** – burning crops, villages, and mills
- **Looting** – seizing valuables, livestock, and prisoners for ransom
- **Psychological terror** – demonstrating the French crown's inability to protect its subjects

### Route & Key Events

1. **Departure from Bordeaux** (4 August 1356)  
   The army – roughly 6,000–8,000 men, including archers, men‑at‑arms, and Gascon allies – marched eastwards.

2. **Raid through Armagnac, Toulouse, and Carcassonne**  
   The force bypassed fortified cities but ravaged the open countryside, causing widespread destruction and panic.

3. **Turning North towards the Loire**  
   After reaching the Mediterranean basin, the Prince turned north, aiming to link up with another English force under the Duke of Lancaster. The French army, hastily assembled, began to shadow him.

4. **The Battle of Poitiers** (19 September 1356)  
   Trapped near the forest of Nouaillé, the outnumbered English took a strong defensive position. Using terrain, dismounted men‑at‑arms, and deadly longbow volleys, they repelled successive French charges. King John II was captured, along with many nobles.

5. **Return to Bordeaux** (2 October 1356)  
   Laden with plunder and the royal prisoner, the Black Prince marched back to Gascony in triumph.

## Impact & Aftermath

- **Political Earthquake** – The capture of a reigning king paralysed the French monarchy, leading to chaos, the **Jacquerie peasant revolt**, and the eventual **Treaty of Brétigny (1360)**.
- **Ransom Crisis** – France had to raise an enormous ransom of 3 million *écus* for John's release.
- **English Prestige** – The Black Prince's reputation soared; Poitiers became a symbol of English military superiority in the 14th century.
- **Shifting Warfare** – The battle reaffirmed the effectiveness of combined longbow tactics against heavy cavalry.
